Four devices, one home

Each does what it does best.

iPad

The wall panel

Runs around the clock. Shows rooms instead of devices, temperature and air, and what the light is drawing right now. After a good minute without a touch it turns into a calm picture: the time and date, how things stand in each room, and whether a light is still on. If your home has an outdoor sensor, its temperature sits right under the clock; if the energy meter is running, the wattage sits below that. Nothing burns in, and nothing glares at night.

iPhone

The remote control

The same picture, in your hand. A tap opens the ring, a drag dims the whole room. Whoever leaves with a light still on gets a notification.

Apple Watch

The dial

The ring fills the screen and the Digital Crown dims the room. It clicks into place, one percent per step — no aiming with your thumb, and your finger hides nothing. The watch face tells you whether a light is still on.

Mac

The window

The same app in a window, on the desk or in the kitchen. It talks to HomeKit on this Mac directly; it does not need an iPhone beside it.

Light

The ring is the control.

How far the arc is closed shows how much light is on in the room. Its colour is the colour your lamps have right now — not at night, when everything stays monochrome so no blue light falls into the room.

  • One tap opens the ring at full size.
  • One drag sets every lamp in the room at once; each percent clicks into place, and you can feel it.
  • A room opened up shows every lamp on its own.

Thirty lamps as a list are a wall of tiles. As rooms they are six rows. In your head it is “there’s a light on in the living room”, not “lamp 3 of 7 is at 72%”.

Energy

What the light costs.

Hue lamps do not measure their own consumption. Lumen Circle works it out, with curves measured on real lamps: from one percent to full blast. A lamp dimmed to half often draws only a third. For known models that is close; when the library does not know a lamp — with Hue plugs that is the rule — the app works with an estimate and says so.

  • Right now: the power draw per room and in total, on the panel and in the screen saver.
  • Day, week, month, year: costs at your electricity price, with high and low tariff if you like.
  • New lamp: the app fetches its profile by itself.
  • If you like: a daily summary in the evening, an alert on high load, a monthly limit in your currency. All three off out of the box.

Press the button on the Hue Bridge once, and that is the whole setup. The measurements come from the open Powercalc project.

Also

What else it does.

Air, before you notice
CO₂ builds up over hours. You get tired long before the air smells stale. Lumen Circle shows the value, places it — from very good to very poor — and tells you that airing the room helps.
Forgot the light?
Whoever leaves with a light still on gets a notification on the iPhone and on the Watch. Needs the location permission set to “Always”. Off out of the box.
Widgets and complications
Is a light still on? The answer is on the Home Screen and on the watch face, without opening the app.
Ten palettes
From Aurora through Graphite to Paper. At night the panel switches to a warm profile by itself.
Large text
Follows the text size from the iOS settings. A panel in the hallway is read by everyone, not only by those with good eyes.
Shortcuts as tiles
Robot vacuum, coffee machine and anything else that does not speak HomeKit: whatever the Shortcuts app can do gets a tile. Five-step guide included.
Wake light
Starts at one percent in deep amber and rises to daylight by the wake time.
Party mode
The light follows the beat. Only the level is measured, right on the device; nothing is recorded.

Setup

Three steps, no account.

  1. Allow access to your home.

    Rooms, names and scenes come from the Home app. The app talks directly to HomeKit, with no sign-in and no service in between.

  2. Press the button on the Hue Bridge.

    That sets the energy meter running. The app finds the bridge on your home network by itself.

  3. iOS asks for the rest when the time comes.

    Local network, notifications, leaving alert, Party mode: each one the first time it is needed. If you say no, you lose exactly that function and nothing else.

What you need is a home in Apple’s Home app. You reach accessories without HomeKit, such as some dishwashers or robot vacuums, through a shortcut.

Privacy

Stays on the device.

There are no servers, and nothing is analysed. Little goes out and nothing personal: the purchase through the App Store, a lamp’s model identifier, “LCT015” for instance, for its consumption profile from the open Powercalc library, and — only if the bridge cannot be found on the home network — an empty request to Signify’s directory service. No name, no identifier for your home, no word on how many lamps you have. The app keeps the spot for the leaving alert on the device, and that is where it stays. The full version.
