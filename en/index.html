Four devices, one home

Each device does exactly what it does best.

iPad

The wall panel

Runs around the clock. It shows you rooms instead of countless individual devices, along with temperature, air quality and what your light is drawing right now. After a minute without a touch the panel turns into a calm display: you see the time, the date and how things stand in each room. If you have an outdoor sensor, its temperature sits right under the clock. If an energy meter is running, you see the current draw. Nothing burns into the screen, and at night nothing glares at all.

iPhone

The remote control

The same familiar picture, right in your hand. A short tap opens the control ring, one swipe dims the light in the whole room. And if you leave with a light still on, a short notification reminds you.

Apple Watch

The dial

The ring fills the whole display, and the Digital Crown becomes the dimmer. Every step clicks into place — one percent per click. No awkward aiming with your thumb, no finger covering half the screen. One look at the watch face is enough to see whether a light is still on somewhere.

Mac

The window

The same app as a practical window on your desk or in the kitchen. It talks to HomeKit on your Mac directly — an iPhone does not have to be lying next to it.

Light control

The ring is the control.

How far the arc is closed shows you at once how bright the room is. The ring glows in exactly the colour of your lamps. Except at night: then the interface stays monochrome, so no disturbing blue light wakes you.

  • One tap opens the ring at full size.
  • One flowing drag sets every lamp in the room at once. Every percent clicks into place.
  • A room opened up shows you every lamp on its own when you need it.

Thirty lamps in a list are a nightmare. Grouped as rooms they are just six clear lines. What you say then is simply “there’s a light on in the living room” instead of “lamp 3 of 7 is at 72%”.

Energy

Transparent down to the last cent.

Philips Hue lamps do not measure their own consumption. Lumen Circle takes that over for you — with real measured curves from one percent up to full brightness. A lamp dimmed to half often draws only a third of the power. For lamps it does not know, such as Hue plugs, the app works with realistic estimates and marks them as such.

  • Live view: the current draw per room and for the whole home — right on the panel and in the screen saver.
  • Cost overview: by day, week, month or year. Worked out at your own electricity price, with high and low tariff if you like.
  • New lamps: the app fetches the matching consumption profile all by itself.
  • Smart alerts (optional): a daily summary in the evening, a warning on unusually high load, or a fixed monthly budget in your currency. Out of the box these alerts are off.

A single press of the button on the Hue Bridge is the whole setup. The measurements come from the open Powercalc project.

Also

What else the app can do.

Air quality in view
The CO₂ level often climbs over hours without anyone noticing. You get tired long before the air smells stale. Lumen Circle places the value — from very good to very poor — and gently reminds you to air the room.
Forgot the light?
Whoever leaves the house gets a notification on the iPhone or the Watch if a light is still on. Needs the location permission set to “Always”, and is off out of the box.
Widgets and complications
Is a light still on somewhere? You see the answer right on the Home Screen or the watch face, without having to open the app.
Ten palettes
From the light “Paper” to the dark “Graphite”. At night the panel switches automatically to a warm profile that is easy on the eyes.
Readable for everyone

Robot vacuum, coffee machine — anything that does not speak HomeKit natively but runs through the Shortcuts app gets a tile of its own. Five-step guide included.
Gentle wake light

Party mode
The light dances to the beat. The level is measured purely on the device — absolutely nothing is recorded.

Setup

Ready in three steps — with no account at all.

  1. Allow access to your home.

    Rooms, names and scenes come straight from the Apple Home app. Lumen Circle talks to HomeKit locally. No sign-in, no external service in between.

  2. Press the button on the Hue Bridge.

    That is all it takes for the energy meter to start counting. The app finds the bridge on your home network automatically.

  3. Permissions as they come up.

    iOS asks you about the local network, notifications or location only when you first use the feature. If you decline, you lose exactly that one function.

What you need is a home set up in the Apple Home app. Accessories without HomeKit you reach through a shortcut.

Privacy

Full privacy.

Your data stays with you. There are no servers, and we analyse nothing at all. The only things that go out are the App Store purchase and your lamp’s model identifier (“LCT015”, for instance) to the open Powercalc library, so the right power profile can be loaded. No name, no address, not even the number of your lamps is transmitted. Even the location for the leaving alert stays stored locally on your device. The full version.
